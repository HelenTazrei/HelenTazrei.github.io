Twenty services, one language

Designing the Services of FintraAccount, a multi-currency accounting platform

CompanyFintranet ↗
ProductFintraAccount ↗
IndustryThe Accounting Platform for Modern Multi-currency Financial Flows

Every money movement in FintraAccount is a Service. People say what happened, and the system keeps the books. My work was making more than twenty different workflows feel like one product, and keeping them right as the product grew. Users moved from spreadsheets to Services, and their feedback was very positive.

A cashier thinks in events. Accounting thinks in entries. The business and the Product Owner defined the accounting behind each workflow. The design challenge was everything people see and do.

The main users of services are exchange staff. Accountants use the product too, and some parts are designed for them. The product handles money, so it must be right. It is used all day, so it must be quick. And it kept changing.

How do you keep twenty-plus workflows simple, consistent and correct while the product keeps changing?

Three moves

1. Design the event, not the entry

Take a cash top-up. The user starts from the customer’s profile, enters a currency and an amount, picks the till or safe, and sees exactly what the account will receive. The entries are created behind the scenes. Every service answers the same three questions: what moved, from where to where, and why? The brief to myself: the smallest, clearest set of inputs.

Left: the form opens with the customer already chosen, and amount and fee stay locked until a currency is picked. Right: the credited amount updates as the user types, and till or safe options narrow to the right currency and branch. An early version; the Discount field was later removed.

2. Give every service the same anatomy

Every service page is built the same way.

Whatever the service, the page is built the same way, and so are shared blocks like agent and payer. People meet familiar ground every time, and a new service starts from a known shape instead of a blank page.

3. One skeleton, different paths

Every service shares the same skeleton. What changes is the path in, shaped by the kind of work and the user’s flow.

Bank servicesStart from a bank activity and are created through allocation.
Exchange and convertInvolve the money entities and currencies on both sides.
Internal transfers (TBA)Move money inside the exchange, combining cash and bank behavior.
Open and close tillA daily cash routine, built as services for consistency.
Purchase and invoiceIncome and expense, built on the same structure.
Manual journalFor the unforeseen. Keeps the service structure, with precise accounting data for expert users.

Hard calls

Edit without breaking the books

THE TENSIONEditing a service, especially data behind its journal entries, was dangerous: one wrong change could upset a whole set of entries. So at first we didn’t allow it.

WHAT WE DECIDEDNon-accounting fields became editable first. After careful study, we found a safe way to allow some accounting fields too.

WHAT IT COSTA clear line between what can change and what can’t, and more rules to explain.

Protect users, don’t block them

THE TENSIONMoney data must be protected, so users can’t enter wrong data. But real exchanges don’t always follow the routine path: a customer may be paid through an acquaintance before their own account is topped up.

WHAT WE DECIDEDWarnings, and in places a second confirmation, so users proceed knowingly. Each conscious decision shows in the log.

WHAT IT COSTA little friction at the moments that matter.

Defaults, not restrictions

THE TENSIONThe long-term idea was to limit each user to their own branch. The current version has no such limit.

WHAT WE DECIDEDPre-fill the branch from where the user works and narrow tills and safes to match, but let them change it.

WHAT IT COSTNo hard guardrail yet. Restricting access is planned for later.

Reuse before reinventing

THE TENSIONThe product needed income and expense tracking in the fastest, simplest way, using what already existed.

WHAT WE DECIDEDBuild purchases and invoices on the same service shell.

WHAT IT COSTOne item per service, and payment handled separately. A known limit, and a project of its own for later.

P2P Top-up: two stages, and a warning instead of a block.

Where design met accounting and engineering

AccountingThe Product Owner specified, per service, which entries must be created.
DesignI checked each form against them, so what the user enters is what the entries need.
EngineeringA service table listed fields, actors and money entities next to the designs.

When the views disagreed, the design changed.

What changed for users

Before Services, users worked in spreadsheets, and everyday work was extremely hard. Without Services there was no product.

Daily work

Faster, and well received

Users’ speed increased a great deal, and their feedback was very positive.

Year-end and reconciliation

Their biggest concern

Complex and time-consuming before. Structured entries, traceable money flow, search and reports made it far more practical.

Growth

Services users asked for

P2P, internal transfer, bank activity and manual journal were added over time, from user feedback.

Building blocks that combine

Because services are building blocks, they combine. In the Fintranet integration, an order creates the services it needs at the right moments, with the user’s approval. FintraAccount remains fully usable without it.
